I've spent years making things for platforms I don't control. Every post lived inside someone else's algorithm, and someone else's rules decided who saw it. This blog is my attempt to do the opposite: one small corner of the internet that is mine, with no feed deciding what gets read.

It's a plain site, hosted for free. I picked that on purpose. If I ever want to say something, I don't need anyone's permission to publish it.

## Why write at all

I'm in my final year of an MSc in Computer Science at Trinity College Dublin, studying intelligent systems. Almost every week I run into an idea that seems worth sharing, such as a thing I learned, a problem I can't stop thinking about, or something about how technology is changing daily life that I'm not sure people are discussing properly. Most of those ideas disappear within a day because I never write them down.

Writing forces me to find out whether I actually understand something. If I can't explain it clearly, I don't understand it yet.

## What to expect

I'll write about three kinds of things:

- **Building.** What I'm making, including the parts that fail. Right now that's a small language model built from scratch, and a dissertation about testing software.
- **Ideas.** Arguments about technology and society that I think could be useful to other people, whether or not they agree with me.
- **Field notes.** What it's like to move from Delhi to Dublin and study AI at the moment it's changing quickly.

## The rules I'm setting for myself

1. Argue about ideas, not people.
2. Say what I know, and say clearly when I'm guessing.
3. Publish often, even when a post isn't perfect.
4. Change my mind in public when I'm wrong.

I'll try to post regularly. If you read something here and it makes you think, or you think I've got it wrong, tell me. That's the point.

Thanks for reading the first one.
